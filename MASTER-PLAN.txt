  PART 3  —  THE STRATEGY, AND WHY
###############################################################################

  THE RECOMMENDATION IN ONE SENTENCE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

     🟡 BE AN H-WEIGHTED, TELEVISION-LED, SELF-FINANCED VOLUME PLAYER
        CONCENTRATED IN MARKETS A AND B, TREATING YEAR 1 AS A CONTROLLED
        EXPERIMENT AND MARKET E AS A YEAR-2 OPTION.

  Here is the reasoning, in the order the arithmetic forces it.


  STEP 1 — CAPACITY IS THE BINDING CONSTRAINT, NOT DEMAND
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     Maximum legal factory (32 lines, 3 shifts, all H) = 44,352,000 units.
     Total H demand = 131,260,000 units.
     You can serve at most 33.8% of one product's market. With SMED and
     preventive maintenance, 40.9%.

     ⇒ You cannot "cover the market". Every plan is a choice about WHICH demand
       to serve. Teams that spread thin to maximise coverage will be beaten by
       teams that concentrate.


  STEP 2 — A AND B ALONE CAN ABSORB EVERYTHING YOU CAN MAKE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     📌 A+B moisturiser demand ......... 56,260,000 units
     📌 Your maximum possible output ... 44,352,000 units
     📌 A+B is 1.27× LARGER than your entire maximum production.

     ⇒ YOU NEVER NEED MARKET E TO FILL YOUR FACTORY. This is the single most
       clarifying fact in the scenario, and it is pure arithmetic.

     What E costs you to enter (§2, §9, §17, §46):
       - Currency risk; the Director resets the FX rate every year
       - 2% customs duty on top of a €0.024/unit export chain
       - 12 sales zones instead of 6, and a 6-phase logistics chain instead of 3
       - Three entrenched local brands (J, K, L) already sitting on E's three
         biggest consumer segments, acting as a single competitor

     🟡 THE HONEST COUNTER-ARGUMENT: you will not win 100% of A+B. With several
        teams competing you might hold 15–25%, which is well under your
        capacity. If that happens, E becomes the place to put idle lines.
        THAT IS A YEAR-2 DECISION, made with year-1 data — not a year-1 guess.


  STEP 3 — WEIGHT HEAVILY TOWARD MOISTURISER
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     H is 3.5× better per euro of capital, 2.4× cheaper per unit, and sits in a
     market 2.3× larger. S needs roughly double the price to break even.

     🟡 This does NOT mean abandon S. Sunscreen genuinely commands a premium and
        a 2-line S presence lets you test premium positioning cheaply. But the
        weight should be H, and the reason is arithmetic, not preference.

     🟢 THE SECOND-ORDER EFFECT MOST TEAMS WILL MISS: sunscreen is the more
        exciting product to build a campaign around, so rivals will over-invest
        in it on instinct. If they crowd into S, the H market gets LESS
        contested — and it was already the bigger one.


  STEP 4 — LEAD WITH TELEVISION
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     TV is the cheapest reach in every market. Social media is the most
     expensive in every market. "Digital-first" is the instinctive modern answer
     and it is wrong here on the scenario's own rate card.

     🟢 Free survey 21 tells you exactly which campaigns every rival ran.


  STEP 5 — FINANCE YOURSELF, AND MAKE THE IDLE CASH WORK
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     You start with €54M and no debt. A sensible year-1 plant costs about €25M,
     leaving roughly €29M. Deposited at 2.5% that surplus earns ~€724,000 a year
     for one decision.
     🟢 Factor receivables at 5.5% rather than borrowing at 10%.
     🔴 Never allow the 20% overdraft — and remember §41 can trigger a loan for
        you automatically.


  STEP 6 — TREAT YEAR 1 AS A CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     The simulator's demand engine is hidden. Nobody — including this model —
     can tell you how many units you will sell at a given price. What you CAN do
     is vary prices and advertising deliberately across markets so that the
     relationship becomes measurable. See PART 8.

  PART 8  —  THE TEST ROUND: FREE INFORMATION
###############################################################################

  Two virtual years, graded ZERO. Most teams will treat it as practice and learn
  nothing measurable. Its real value is that experiments cost nothing.

  🔵 RUN THESE EIGHT EXPERIMENTS

     1. CHARGE DIFFERENT PRICES IN A AND B.
        Without price variation, no method on earth can recover a price
        elasticity. This is the single most valuable experiment.

     2. VARY ADVERTISING SPEND SHARPLY BETWEEN MARKETS.
        Gives you the advertising elasticity — how much reach converts.

     3. DELIBERATELY STOCK OUT IN ONE MARKET.
        Measures what §34's "brand prestige" damage actually costs. No other
        team will know this number.

     4. RUN ONE ZONE OWN-MANAGED AND ONE VIA WHOLESALERS.
        Gives the true breakeven, not the theoretical one.

     5. TRY 2 SHIFTS IN YEAR 1, 3 SHIFTS IN YEAR 2.
        Tells you whether the extra output beats the depreciation step from
        10% to 12%.

     6. TEST ONE PROMOTION TYPE PER CHANNEL.
        Reveals which promotions the simulator actually accepts.

     7. BUY THE €11,000 SURVEY BUNDLE BOTH YEARS.
        Builds a rival baseline before it counts.

     8. COMPARE ACTUAL OUTPUT AGAINST capacity().
        Settles the §24 module-counter reading and the §42 loan repayment rule.

  🔵 Record every year as an Observation and run fit_elasticities(). By the
     graded round you FORECAST from your own data instead of guessing — which is
     exactly what the rubric rewards.
