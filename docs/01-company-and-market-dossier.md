# Doc 01 — Company & Market Dossier

**Everything about the company and its market, in the order you'll make decisions.**
Every figure carries its scenario section (`§`). Nothing is invented.

> **Read this first:** MMT39 is not a real company. Every team is handed the *identical* business on
> day one — same balance sheet, no products, no brand, no debt (§1). There is no incumbent advantage
> to find. So "our weaknesses" means **the traps in the rules**, and "our edge" means **doing the
> arithmetic the other teams skip**.

**Verdict markers used throughout:**

| In tables | In text | Meaning                                             |
| --------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| `[!]`     | ⚠️      | **Trap** — this costs you real money if you miss it |
| `[+]`     | ✅       | **Edge** — free or cheap advantage most teams skip  |
| `[~]`     | ⚖️      | **Judgement call** — genuinely depends on strategy  |

## 1. The company

| Opening balance sheet (§1)                            | €              |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Fixed assets (land, buildings, production facilities) | 12,000,000     |
| Inventory                                             | 0              |
| Cash & equivalents                                    | 54,000,000     |
| **= Equity**                                          | **66,000,000** |

No debt. No products. No brand. Headquarters in the EU, reporting in euros (§2).

**What you actually control:** a factory site with room for 32 production lines, and €54M of cash.
That's it. Everything else you build.

⚠️ **Equity can never be increased (§37).** You cannot raise money from shareholders. Your only
outside funding is bank debt and supplier credit. The €54M is all the free capital you will ever get.

✅ **82% of your balance sheet is idle cash earning nothing.** At the 2.5% deposit rate (§44, §49)
that is **€1,350,000 a year** you're giving away by leaving it in the Cash account.

**Buyers are 25% of population**, split 50/50 male/female (§5). That's your advertising target, not

### Market size in units per year (§3 × §2)

|                     | A          | B          | E          | **Total**       |

| **H** — moisturiser | 29,260,000 | 27,000,000 | 75,000,000 | **131,260,000** |

**H is 2.3× the size of S.** E alone is 62% of S demand and 57% of H demand.

### Distribution channels (§4)

| Channel                                       | A     | B     | E   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----- | ----- | --- |
| **T** — traditional (pharmacies, perfumeries) | 2,160 | 1,080 | —   |

| **G** — hypermarkets                          | 35    | 30    | 200 |

**E has only hypermarkets.** 200 stores, no salesforce needed — the sales manager handles channel G
directly (§6). E is operationally *simpler* to sell into than A or B, despite being foreign.

### Verdict on market E

⚖️ **E is where the volume is and where the difficulty is.** This is the central strategic question.

| For E                                        | Against E                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 62% of S demand, 57% of H demand             | Local brands J, K and L already sit on the three biggest segments and act as one competitor (§17) |
| Richest consumers ($46,000/head)             | Currency risk — the Director sets the FX rate each year (§46)                                     |
| Only one channel to serve (200 hypermarkets) | 2% customs duty on top of a €0.024/unit export chain (§9)                                         |
| No salesforce recruitment needed (§6)        | 12 sales zones instead of 6, and a 6-phase logistics chain instead of 3 (§9)                      |

⚠️ **A and B have no competition except the other student teams.** E adds a domestic incumbent that
never goes away.

## 3. The products — and the biggest decision in the game

Both are mature, high-turnover personal care products with long shelf life. Inventory never spoils,
and oldest stock sells first (§3).

| Per production line                | **S** (sunscreen)            | **H** (moisturiser)          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Cost to install                    | €2,100,000                   | €1,500,000                   |
| Nameplate capacity/shift/year      | 240,000                      | 600,000                      |
| **Sellable at 3 shifts**           | **554,400**                  | **1,386,000**                |
| **Capital per unit of capacity**   | **€3.79**                    | **€1.08**                    |
| **Standard cost per unit**         | **€5.13**                    | **€2.13**                    |
| Break-even price (own salesforce)  | €7.99                        | €3.86                        |
| Break-even price (via wholesalers) | €8.77                        | €4.23                        |
| Crew per line per shift            | 9 specialists + 32 operators | 6 specialists + 23 operators |

*§22, §23, §30, §33, §49. Standard cost includes raw material, power, labour at minimum legal wage,
scrap and breakdown repair.*

### ⚠️ The trap

**Lines are irreversible.** Once installed, a line stays until the end of the simulation — you can
switch it off but never remove it, and never convert an S line to an H line (§22). **Your year-1
product mix is locked for all four graded years.**

### The verdict

**H is 3.5× more capital-efficient and 2.4× cheaper to run.** Sunscreen needs roughly *double* the
retail price to break even.

That doesn't make S wrong — sunscreen genuinely commands a premium in the real world, and the
scenario prices reflect that. But S must earn its higher price through positioning and brand, or it
loses money. It is the more exciting product to build a campaign around, which is exactly why some
rivals will over-invest in it on instinct rather than arithmetic.

## 4. The factory

### ⚠️ A fifth of your capacity does not exist

Per line, per shift, per year (§23, §24):

|                                                 | Hours     |            |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| Scheduled                                       | 2,000     |            |
| − breakdown repair (70 breakdowns × 2.5 h)      | −175      | −8.8%      |
| − main-module servicing (every 40 h, takes 5 h) | −250      | −12.5%     |
| **= Actually producing**                        | **1,575** | **78.75%** |

**The §23 headline capacities are nameplate, not achievable.** Plan off them and you over-commit by
21%, stock out, and take the §34 brand-loyalty damage — which persists into later years.

### The four productivity investments

| System                           | Cost                         | Effect                                                   | Payback                                     | Verdict                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Preventive maintenance** (§25) | €500,000/yr                  | Foresees 70% of breakdowns                               | ~€613k benefit at 10 H lines/3 shifts       | [+] **Yes** once the plant is large. Reversible — decided annually |
| **SMED** (§27)                   | €3,000,000/module            | Removes all 250 h of module downtime → **+15.9% output** | 1.8 to 14.4 years — *depends on an unknown* | [~] **Ask the Director first** (see below)                         |
| **Poka Yoke** (§26)              | €720,000 **per line**        | Removes scrap, cuts detection time → +4.55%              | **12.3 years**                              | [!] **No.** Payback exceeds the 4-year game                        |
| **Extra shifts** (§23)           | Labour + higher depreciation | 2× or 3× output from the same line                       | Immediate                                   | [+] Usually yes — spreads irreversible capex over more units       |

### ⚖️ The SMED question you must ask in Session 2

SMED is worth €208,614 of extra profit per line per year. Whether to buy it depends entirely on one
thing the scenario never states: **how many production lines can a single duplicate module serve?**

| Lines per module | Payback    | Verdict     |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ----------- |
| 1                | 14.4 years | Never       |
| 4                | 3.6 years  | Marginal    |
| 8                | 1.8 years  | Clearly yes |

The physics hints at up to 8 — servicing takes 5 hours and recurs every 40 operating hours — but
that's inference, not a rule. **This is the highest-value unknown in the game.**

⚠️ **SMED cannot be bought in year 1** (§27) — the factory must run a year first. So your year-1 plan
must assume 78.75% capacity, not a fix you can't yet purchase.

⚠️ **Both SMED and Poka Yoke are irreversible, and Poka Yoke applies to every line you own now and
every line you ever add** (§26). Buying it early with 20 lines planned means €14.4M, not €720k.

### Depreciation rises with shifts (§35)

| Shifts | Depreciation on total fixed assets |
| ------ | ---------------------------------- |
| 1      | 8%                                 |
| 2      | 10%                                |
| 3      | 12%                                |

Three shifts is usually right, but it is not free.

### Raw materials (§28)

One "raw material unit" = everything needed to make one finished unit. Cost: **S €1.65, H €1.05**
(§49, Director may change). You negotiate on volume and payment terms (0 / 60 / 90 / 120 days).

⚠️ **Under-order and you pay +15% on the shortfall.** And you must order for units *used*, not units
*produced* — scrap consumes raw material too (§34).

## 5. Getting to market

### Own sales managers vs. wholesalers (§6, §7, §11)

You choose, per market, how many of the sales zones you staff yourself. The rest go to independent
wholesalers. Mixed is allowed. **Every zone must be covered one way or the other.**

|                                      | Own sales managers                                    | Wholesalers                                    |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Cost                                 | €50,000/manager/yr (A, B); $60,000 (E) + rep salaries | **8.46%** of retail price (6.5% on promotions) |
| Who pays platform storage/handling   | **You**                                               | Them                                           |
| Who pays platform→retailer transport | **You**                                               | Them                                           |
| Who pays E customs duty              | **You**                                               | Them                                           |

⚠️ **The wholesaler cut is a percentage of retail price**, so it scales with your price. Combined
with the automatic 4.5% R&D charge, **roughly 13% of every price increase never reaches you.** That's
why the H break-even jumps from €3.86 to €4.23 just by switching to wholesalers.

Use `distribution_compare()` in the model — the breakeven depends on your volume per zone.

### Logistics (§8–§12)

You rent vehicles. Cost formula: **24,000 + 1.2 × load capacity in kg**. One kilogram = two units.
A vehicle runs 2,000 hours a year; factory→platform routes average 8 hours.

| Decision                        | Range                                                  |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Platforms per market            | A/B: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 · E: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72 |
| Sales managers                  | A/B: 0–6 · E: 0–12                                     |
| Vehicle load, factory→platform  | 1,000–24,000 kg                                        |
| Vehicle load, platform→retailer | 500–10,000 kg                                          |

✅ **The optimum has an exact answer.** Once your fleet covers your volume, cost collapses to
`24,000 × vehicles + 0.0024 × units` — the per-kilogram term cancels. So **minimise the number of
vehicles, then size each to fit your volume exactly.** Use `optimal_vehicle_plan()`.

⚠️ **§10 offers €0.006/unit as "reasonable." It's a decoy.** A full 24,000 kg vehicle achieves
€0.0044. Any team that hits the benchmark pays **36% over** while believing it optimised.

⚠️ **You pay factory→platform transport in every zone** — including zones run by wholesalers (§8).
Only the last mile is theirs.

⚠️ **Zero is not a legal entry in any logistics field** (§12). Leave one blank and the plan breaks.

### Retailers (§20, §21)

- **Retailer margin** is set in € per unit, per market, per channel, per product. Price minus margin
  = what you charge the retailer. Paid immediately (§38).
- **Shelf space** is an *objective*, not a setting. You state a target %; the simulator negotiates on
  the retailer's behalf and returns a different actual %.

⚠️ **Over-asking on shelf space backfires** — §21 explicitly says the retailer "would react
negatively." Ask for feasible growth.

⚠️ **No price, no margin, or no shelf space = the simulator assumes you are not selling in that
channel at all** (§14, §20, §21). A blank field is a silent exit from a market.

## 6. Creating demand

### Advertising (§15, §16)

| Medium  | A/B rate | A/B reach | **€ per 1% reached** | E rate  | E reach | **$ per 1%**  |
| ------- | -------- | --------- | -------------------- | ------- | ------- | ------------- |
| **TV**  | €18,000  | 60%       | **300.0** [+]        | $20,000 | 61%     | **327.9** [+] |
| Press   | €15,000  | 48%       | 312.5                | $16,000 | 31%     | 516.1         |
| Display | €10,000  | 28%       | 357.1                | $11,000 | 26%     | 423.1         |
| Radio   | €14,000  | 35%       | 400.0                | $16,000 | 40%     | 400.0         |
| Social  | €15,000  | 30%       | 500.0 [!]            | $15,000 | 29%     | 517.2 [!]     |

✅ **Television is cheapest in both markets. Social media is the most expensive in both** — 67% worse
than TV in A/B, 58% worse in E.

**Scope is reach per insertion; extra insertions buy frequency, not new audience.** Media overlap, so
reaching 90% of buyers in A costs €57,000 across four media, versus €18,000 for 60% from TV alone.

⚠️ "Digital-first, social-led" is the instinctive modern answer and it is wrong on the scenario's own
numbers.

### Brand positioning (§17)

- 20 sunscreen campaigns and 20 moisturiser campaigns to choose from (§51).
- **Three consumer segments, weighted 40% / 40% / 20%.** You must locate them yourself.
- Positioning maps come from market research (surveys 7 and 8, €59,900 each) with **unlabelled axes**
  you have to interpret.
- In E, local brands **J, K and L** sit on the three best segments.

✅ **Positioning can be changed every year at zero cost and with no extra marketing spend** (§17).
This is unusual and most teams won't notice. It makes year-1 positioning a cheap experiment, not a
commitment.

⚠️ **No campaign selected = the simulator assigns you a neutral, undefined position** (§17).

### Promotions (§18)

Max **3 per channel, per product, per year**. Effect lasts **3 of 52 weeks**. Only one type per
channel per year. Rejected promotions cost nothing — except drawings.

| Type                      | Cost per unit at €6.00 retail |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Direct gift (buy 3)       | €0.40 (6.7%)                  |
| Price reduction €0.50     | €0.50 (8.3%)                  |
| Discount on next purchase | €0.50 (8.3%)                  |
| Indirect gift (buy 3)     | €1.20 (20.0%)                 |
| **3×2**                   | **€2.00 (33.3%)** [!]         |

⚠️ **A 3×2 is a 33% discount.** It looks like a promotion; it prices like a fire sale.

⚠️ **If you can't supply the demand a promotion creates, the market rejects the promotion** (§48).
Promoting beyond capacity doesn't just stock you out — it wastes the promotion entirely.

*Note: §18 says "eight types" then lists six. Ask the Director.*

### Point of purchase (§19)

Budget for posters and display stands, set separately for S and H. No formula given — benchmark
against real-world practice.

## 8. Money

| Instrument                                | Rate (§49)                          | Verdict                              |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Fixed-term deposit / financial investment | **2.5% earned**                     | [+] Park surplus cash here           |
| **Factoring**                             | **5.5% paid**                       | [+] Cheapest way to fund receivables |
| Credit line                               | 7.0% + 1% service + 1% upfront      | [~] Short-term gaps only             |
| Bank loan                                 | 10.0% + 1% upfront, 4-year maturity | [~] For capex, not working capital   |
| **Overdraft**                             | **20.0%**                           | [!] Never                            |

### The three financing conclusions

✅ **Factor, don't borrow.** On €100M revenue at 120-day terms the receivable is €33.3M; factoring at
5.5% instead of a 10% loan saves **€1,500,000 a year**.

✅ **Deposit idle cash.** €54M × 2.5% = **€1,350,000 a year**, free.

⚠️ **Never let an overdraft happen — and it can happen passively.** §41 says if your Operating Cash
Needed exceeds available cash, *the simulator takes a loan for you*. Sloppy cash planning is silently
expensive.

### The OCN rule you must check before every submission (§41)

> **OCN = 80% of new capex + 50% of (advertising + POP + sales managers + rep fixed salaries +
> overhead + market research) + 15% of raw material cost**

Compare against cash on 2 January = cash on 31 Dec − loan repayments due − credit lines due −
payables + investments + deposits + receivables. Use `ocn()` in the model.

### Other finance facts

- **Customer terms:** 60, 90 or 120 days — same for all markets, channels and products (§38).
- **R&D is 4.5% of revenue, automatic and unavoidable** (§36). A direct tax on premium pricing.
- **A loan drawn this year is not repaid this year** — it leaves your cash on 2 January (§47).
- **You may buy shares in 3 real listed companies** with surplus cash (§45). Rarely the best use.
- ⚠️ **Cash can go negative, and then a loan is compulsory** (§42).

## 11. The ten things to remember

1. **You can serve about a third of the market at maximum size.** 32 H lines at 3 shifts = 44.4M units
   against 131.3M of H demand. Capacity is the constraint, not demand.
2. **Real capacity is 78.75% of the headline number.** Never plan off §23's nameplate figures.
3. **H is 3.5× more capital-efficient and 2.4× cheaper than S.** And the choice is locked in year 1.
4. **Television is the cheapest reach in every market.** Social media is the most expensive.
5. **Minimise vehicle count, then size to fit.** The €0.006 benchmark costs 36% too much.
6. **Factor receivables (5.5%), never borrow for working capital (10%), never overdraft (20%).**
7. **Deposit your idle cash.** €1.35M a year for one decision.
8. **Three surveys are free.** Rival prices, campaigns and promotions, every year, for nothing.
9. **Poka Yoke is a trap. SMED depends on a question you must ask the Director.**
10. **Check every field before submitting.** A blank price, margin or shelf space means zero sales.
