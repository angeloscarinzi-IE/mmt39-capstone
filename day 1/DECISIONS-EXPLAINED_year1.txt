  WHY IT IS SO LOPSIDED
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     The wholesaler charges a PERCENTAGE of retail price, so their cost rises
     with both your volume and your price. Your own network is almost entirely
     FIXED — a EUR 50,000 manager plus rep salaries that do not move with units.
     At high volume the percentage fee dwarfs the fixed cost.

     🔴 The wholesaler quietly punishes success. Every euro you add to price
        hands them 8.46 cents more — on top of the automatic 4.5% R&D charge.
        Roughly 13% of any price increase never reaches you.

  WHERE IT FLIPS
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

     | % of plan sold | Units/zone | Winner     | Gap         |
     | -------------- | ---------- | ---------- | ----------- |
     | 100%           | 1,241,750  | OWN        | EUR 224,699 |
     | 60%            |   745,050  | OWN        | EUR  58,424 |
     | 50%            |   620,875  | OWN        | EUR  16,856 |
     | 45%            |   558,787  | WHOLESALER | EUR   3,928 |
     | 30%            |   372,525  | WHOLESALER | EUR  66,281 |

     📌 BREAKEVEN IS 45% OF PLANNED VOLUME. You would have to sell less than
        half of what the plant makes before wholesalers win — and even then the
        gap is EUR 3,928 a zone, trivial.

  🔴 DO NOT HEDGE WITH A MIX
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     Tested every split from 0 to 6 managers. At planned volume EVERY wholesaler
     zone you add costs EUR 224,699. The zones are independent, so partial
     mixing simply buys a fraction of the wrong answer — there is no
     diversification benefit.

     Even in the downside case (45% of plan), all-wholesaler beats all-own by
     only EUR 23,570 across a whole market. The upside is ~100x the downside.

  🔵 FOR SATURDAY (test round) — HOLD THIS CONSTANT
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     You get two virtual years and can only cleanly test two things.
     DISTRIBUTION SHOULD NOT BE ONE OF THEM. Its cost side is fully computable
     — the table above IS the answer. The only genuine unknown is whether
     wholesaler zones sell differently, and nothing in the scenario suggests so.

     Keep 6 managers in A and 6 in B, identical across both test years, so the
     advertising experiment (A full spend, B half) actually isolates the
     advertising response. Vary two things at once and you learn neither.

  🟡 HONEST CAVEAT ON REP COUNT
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     Cost falls monotonically with fewer reps — 3/zone is EUR 52,000 cheaper
     than 5/zone. But reps GENERATE the sales in channels S and T. The model can
     price them; it cannot tell you how many units each one sells, because the
     scenario never says.

     The counts above are scaled to outlet density (A: 270 supermarkets + 360
     traditional per zone; B: 180 + 180), NOT optimised. Treat it as a cost
     floor and a candidate for a year-2 test.

     ⬜ Market E needs NO reps at all — it has only channel G, which the sales
        manager handles directly (S6).

  A. HOW MANY PLATFORMS?  ANSWER: 6 — THE LEGAL MINIMUM

     🟢 PLATFORM COUNT IS BARELY A COST DECISION AT ALL. Going from 6 to 36
        costs an extra EUR 1,014 a year — EUR 0.000136 per unit.

     WHY, from S11: storage is proportional to average STOCK, delivery notes to
     the number of RETAILERS, and handling to UNITS SOLD. All three are
     independent of how many platforms you split them across. Only the EUR 0.65
     order-to-factory charge scales, at EUR 33.80 per platform per year.

     🟡 So choose on SERVICE, not cost. More platforms mean shorter last-mile
        routes:
             6 platforms  -> 636 retailers each in market A
            36 platforms  -> 106 retailers each

        Start at 6. If your actual platform-to-retailer cost comes back above
        the EUR 0.007 benchmark, raising platform count is the cheapest lever
        you have — it costs almost nothing.

     🔴 THE TEMPLATE DEFAULT IS 30 PLATFORMS. Harmless on cost, but it signals
        the template is illustrative, not a recommendation. Do not inherit it.

  B. FACTORY -> PLATFORM.  ANSWER: ONE VEHICLE, SIZED TO FIT
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     This leg is EXACT. S10 fixes the average route at 8 hours, so a vehicle
     runs 2,000/8 = 250 routes a year and carries load_kg x 2 units per route.

     Market A, 7,450,498 units:

     | Vehicles | Load kg | Annual capacity | Cost       | Per unit |
     | -------- | ------- | --------------- | ---------- | -------- |
     | 1        | 14,901  | 7,450,500       | EUR 41,881 | 0.00562  |
     | 2        |  7,451  | 7,451,000       | EUR 65,882 | 0.00884  |
     | 3        |  4,967  | 7,450,500       | EUR 89,881 | 0.01206  |
     | 4        |  3,726  | 7,452,000       | EUR 113,885| 0.01529  |

     📌 THE ALGEBRA THAT SETTLES IT
        Size the fleet to exactly cover volume:  kg = units / (2 x 250 x n)
        Substitute into the S10 cost formula 24,000 + 1.2 x kg:

              TOTAL COST  =  24,000 x n  +  0.0024 x units

        The per-kilogram term is INVARIANT — it cancels out completely. Only the
        VEHICLE COUNT matters. Every extra vehicle is a flat EUR 24,000 that
        buys you nothing.

     🟢 So the rule is: USE THE FEWEST VEHICLES THAT CAN CARRY THE VOLUME, then
        size each one exactly to fit. One vehicle at 24,000 kg tops out at
        12,000,000 units a year; above that you need a second.

     🔴 Note this is NOT "always buy the biggest vehicle". Buying 24,000 kg when
        14,901 kg would fit wastes 1.2 x 9,099 = EUR 10,919 for nothing.

     🟡 THIS LEG IS A GENUINE UNKNOWN, AND I WILL NOT PRETEND OTHERWISE.
        Unlike factory-to-platform, S12 does NOT fix the route time — it says
        explicitly "the average route time can be any". The scenario never gives
        retailer spacing or drop density, so the geometry has nothing under it.

        I built a geometric estimator following the method S8 alludes to. It
        returns route times over 100 hours and a cost roughly 20x the benchmark.
        That is the estimator failing, not a finding. It is kept in the model
        only with a loud warning attached.

     🔵 SO SIZE IT FROM THE BENCHMARK AND CORRECT FROM REALITY.
        S12 says EUR 0.007 per unit is reasonable. One 10,000 kg vehicle lands
        just under that. Submit it, then read two things from your results:

           · A LOGISTIC STOCKOUT flag  -> too few vehicles. The logistics company
             covered you at a higher price, so you lost money but NOT a sale.
             Increase vehicles or capacity.
           · COST BELOW BENCHMARK with NO stockout -> over-provisioned. Cut back.

        S8 endorses exactly this trial-and-correct loop. Getting it wrong on
        Saturday costs nothing; that is what the test round is for.

     ⬜ Last-mile vehicles run ONLY in zones you manage yourself (S12). Since
        Section 1 puts your own managers in all 12 zones, you need them in both
        A and B. If you had used wholesalers anywhere, those zones would need
        none — the wholesaler's 8.46% covers it.
