  PART 2  —  THE YEAR 2 STRATEGY:  ENTER MARKET E AND BUILD OUT
###############################################################################

  This is a TEST RUN, so we use it to run the experiment that matters most:
  ENTER MARKET E AND EXPAND THE FACTORY TO THE 32-LINE LIMIT.

  THE LOGIC, IN ORDER

  1. WE ARE CAPACITY-BOUND, NOT DEMAND-BOUND. Year 1 demand was 40,385,229
     units against 14,430,000 of output. Entering E without adding capacity
     would just move units between markets. So E and expansion go together or
     not at all.

  2. E PAYS MORE PER UNIT THAN A OR B. Income there is $46,000 against
     EUR 27,000 in A and EUR 35,000 in B, so it carries higher prices even after
     the export chain and customs:

     | Line        | Price | Margin | Contribution per unit |
     | ----------- | ----- | ------ | --------------------- |
     | H market A  |  5.35 |   0.95 |                 1.671 |
     | H market B  |  5.70 |   0.95 |                 2.005 |
     | H market E  |  6.90 |   1.80 |                 2.226 |
     | S market A  | 15.60 |   1.90 |                 6.679 |
     | S market B  | 16.50 |   1.90 |                 7.539 |
     | S market E  | 19.50 |   3.60 |                 8.553 |

     Export costs are small: EUR 0.024/unit plus 2% customs = EUR 0.0743 on H
     and EUR 0.1509 on S. E also needs NO SALES REPS — it has only channel G,
     200 hypermarkets, handled by the sales manager (S6).

  3. LINES PAY BACK IN UNDER SIX MONTHS AT THE NEW PRICES.
        1 S line: 670,860 units x EUR 6.679 = EUR 4,480,805/yr on EUR 2,100,000
                  -> PAYBACK 0.47 YEARS
        1 H line: 1,677,150 units x EUR 1.671 = EUR 2,801,684/yr on EUR 1,500,000
                  -> PAYBACK 0.54 YEARS

  4. THE YEAR-1 MISTAKE PAYS FOR THE H EXPANSION. We hold 116,677,000 units of H
     raw material. The expanded plant needs 40,478,400. WE BUY NONE, and still
     have 76,198,600 left over. The over-order that wrecked Year 1 is what makes
     Year 2's expansion affordable.

  🔴 WHY THIS IS SAFE EVEN IF E DISAPPOINTS

     | E capture | E H units  | E S units | Revenue     | Net income  | Unsold     |
     | --------- | ---------- | --------- | ----------- | ----------- | ---------- |
     | 100%      | 15,934,087 |   784,142 | 338,622,637 | 101,124,726 |          0 |
     | 75%       | 11,950,565 |   588,106 | 307,313,646 |  90,253,394 |  4,179,557 |

     | 25%       |  3,983,522 |   196,035 | 244,695,666 |  68,510,730 | 12,538,671 |
     | 0%        |          0 |         0 | 213,386,675 |  57,639,399 | 16,718,229 |

     📌 EVEN IF WE SELL NOTHING AT ALL IN E, net income is about EUR 57,639,399
        against Year 1's EUR 4,919,135. The A+B price rises and the third shift
        carry the plan on their own. Anything E delivers is upside.
     📌 Unsold units become finished-goods inventory. They never deteriorate and
        are sold first the following year (S3). THE DOWNSIDE IS WORKING CAPITAL,
        NOT LOSS — which is exactly what makes this a sound experiment.

  🟡 THE TWO ASSUMPTIONS I CANNOT GROUND
     · Price elasticity of -1.5. Used to predict how far A+B demand falls when we
       raise prices. Not measured, assumed.
     · A 21.2% share of E's moisturiser market in our first year there, against
       three entrenched local brands (J, K, L) and any other team that enters.
       Optimistic. The table above is why being wrong is survivable.
