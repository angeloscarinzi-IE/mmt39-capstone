  ENTRY 1 — THE DECISION MODEL          model/mmt39.py
===============================================================================

  WHAT IT IS
  2,650 lines of Python. Zero dependencies. 89 self-tests passing.
  You give it decisions; it tells you what you can make, what it costs, and
  what your income statement and balance sheet will look like.

  FIVE COMMANDS YOU CAN RUN TODAY

      python3 model/mmt39.py --selftest      89 checks against the rulebook
      python3 model/mmt39.py --briefing      every headline number for a deck
      python3 model/mmt39.py --demo          worked year: P&L, BS, ratios
      python3 model/mmt39.py --promotions 6  what each promotion type costs
      python3 model/mmt39.py --breakeven 2.13 0.95 0.60

  THREE THINGS THAT CAME OUT OF BUILDING IT

  1. The break-even calculator revealed a pricing trap.
     Going from your own sales managers to full wholesalers pushes the minimum
     viable price from EUR 3.53 to EUR 3.87 - a 10% jump. The wholesaler takes
     8.46% OF RETAIL PRICE, so every euro you add hands 8.46 cents straight
     back. Combined with the automatic 4.5% R&D charge, about 13% of every
     price increase never reaches you.

  2. The demo run failed on purpose, and that is useful.
     Given deliberately mediocre decisions it lost money both years, flagged an
     S-line stockout, and forced a EUR 3.1M loan when cash went negative. That
     is the model behaving correctly - and a preview of what happens to teams
     that over-invest in sunscreen lines.

  3. The demand fitter passed a test it could not cheat.
     Fed fabricated data generated from a price elasticity of -1.8 that it was
     never told, it recovered -1.800. Once Year-1 results land it will extract
     your real elasticities. It also REFUSES to fit when you have not varied
     your prices - which is exactly why the ungraded round matters.

  TWO HONEST LIMITATIONS

  - Section 18 says "eight types of promotion" then lists six. The six
    described are modelled. Ask the Director.
  - Section 42 says loan repayment is "a proportion" of the opening balance but
    never defines the proportion. Both readings are implemented; the default is
    the one that actually clears the debt. Check your first balance sheet
    against both.

  Wherever the scenario is silent on physics (repair-time savings, last-mile
  route geometry, average stock levels) it is marked ASSUMPTION in the code
  with the reasoning, rather than presenting a guess as fact.

  THE PROBLEM
  Four tables had pipes that did not line up - every one contained an emoji.
  Emoji occupy two columns on screen but count as one or two characters. You
  cannot align pipes in both the raw file and a rendered viewer at once.

  THE FIX, IN THREE STEPS
      1. Wrote a table formatter padding every cell to a common width
         -> 34 tables reflowed
      2. Replaced emoji INSIDE tables with [!] [+] [~]
         -> 25 replacements; every character now exactly one column
      3. Switched padding from display-width to character count
         -> pipes align exactly in the raw file

  Emoji still appear in prose and headings, where alignment does not matter.

  VERIFIED PROGRAMMATICALLY: compared the exact character position of every
  pipe in every row against its header row. 35 tables, 0 misaligned, no wide
  characters remaining in any cell. The formatter is saved and runs over each

  THREE THINGS WORTH PULLING OUT

  1. THE SUNSCREEN TRAP HAS A SECOND HALF.
     Everyone will notice S is 3.5x more capital-hungry. What they will miss is
     the consequence: if rivals crowd into sunscreen on instinct, THE
     MOISTURISER MARKET GETS LESS CONTESTED, NOT MORE - and it is already 2.3x
     bigger (131.3M vs 56.2M units). The crowd moving one way makes the other
     way better.

  2. STOCKOUTS ARE A TWO-SIDED EVENT.
     Section 34 says a stockout damages brand loyalty into later years. So when
     a rival plans off nameplate capacity and stocks out in year 1, their
     year-2 demand is already impaired before year 2 starts. Your counter is
     not just avoiding your own stockout - it is being ready to absorb their
     share.

  3. THE UNGRADED ROUND IS THE LARGEST FREE EDGE IN THE COURSE.
     It is graded zero, so most teams will treat it as practice. Its real value
     is that it is the only place you can run experiments that cost nothing.
     Section 4 lists eight - deliberately varying prices between markets,
     deliberately stocking out once to measure what the brand damage actually
     costs. Without that variation the demand fitter cannot recover a price
     elasticity, and no method can.

  ONE CAVEAT ON SECTION 3
  The predicted rival behaviours are HYPOTHESES, NOT FACTS, and are labelled
  that way in the doc. They are reasoned from what the scenario rewards versus
  what reads as intuitive. Verify them with the free surveys after year 1
  before betting more capital on them.

  1. DUMPING IS BANNED — "you cannot sell below production cost plus the
     retailer margin." Floor is EUR 3.08 for H, EUR 7.03 for S. Note that

     binds. The dumping rule only matters in a price war.
  2. POINT-OF-SALE ADVERTISING IS CAPPED at EUR 500,000 per channel per year.
  3. LOAN TERMS SHORTEN each simulation year. Borrowing late is repaid faster.

  ONE THING I CORRECTED WHILE BUILDING IT

     then correct from year-1 actuals — and left a loud warning on the old
     function. This is the honest answer, not the impressive-looking one.

     productivity decision. With PM on, Year-1 output is 774,480 S and
     13,553,400 H, and standard cost falls to EUR 4.93 / EUR 2.06.
